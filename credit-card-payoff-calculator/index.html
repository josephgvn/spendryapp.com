A short guide to credit card debt

How credit card interest is calculated

Each month your card charges interest on the balance you carry, based on its APR. Your payment covers that interest first, and the rest reduces the balance. The calculator repeats this month by month until the balance is gone.

Example: a $3,000 balance at 24% APR, which is 2% a month, is charged $60 of interest. Pay $200 and the balance drops to $2,860; next month's interest is worked out on that new amount.

Why minimum payments take so long

The minimum payment is usually a small percentage of the balance. As the balance falls, so does the minimum, so most of each payment goes to interest and the debt can last for years.

Paying a fixed amount breaks that cycle: the same money keeps shrinking the balance every month. The comparison in the calculator shows the difference with your numbers.

Statement date and due date

The statement date closes the billing cycle and sets your balance. The due date is the last day to pay it, usually at least 21 days later.

Pay the full statement balance by the due date and you are not charged interest on purchases. Carry part of it and interest is charged on what is left.

Ways to pay off a card faster

  • Pay a fixed amount each month, as high as you can, instead of the minimum.
  • Pause new spending on the card while you pay it down.
  • If you have several cards, put the extra toward the highest APR first.
  • Check whether a balance transfer or a lower-rate loan really costs less once fees are included.
  • Never miss a due date; late fees and penalty rates push your plan back.

Frequently asked questions

How long will it take if I only pay the minimum?

It depends on your balance, your APR and how your card works out the minimum, but it often takes years. The calculator shows when the balance would be gone with minimum payments only and how much interest that costs.

What is APR?

APR is your card's yearly interest rate. Cards charge interest monthly, at roughly one twelfth of the APR, on the balance you carry.

How is the minimum payment worked out?

Most cards take a small percentage of the balance, often with a fixed floor, plus interest and fees. Your statement shows yours; you can change the percentage in the calculator.

Does the calculator include fees?

No. Annual fees, late fees and balance transfer fees are not included, so your real cost can be a little higher.

Is anything I enter saved?

No. The math runs entirely in your browser; nothing you enter is saved or sent anywhere.

How does Spendry track my card debt?

You add your cards; Spendry follows their statement and due dates, reminds you before payments and updates your balance and debt-free date as you record payments. It never asks for your bank login.
