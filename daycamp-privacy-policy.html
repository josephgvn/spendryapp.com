Daycamp

Privacy Policy

Effective November 14, 2026

The short version

Daycamp does not collect, sell or share personal data. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. What you enter stays on your iPhone. The one thing that ever leaves it is an online food search you start yourself, described below.

Data kept on your iPhone

Your habits, challenges and goals and their history; journal entries with their photos, mood, tags and the things you are grateful for; tasks; focus sessions; food, water and weight entries; the places and automation rules you set up; your camp, character and rewards; and your settings.

All of it is stored in Daycamp's private storage on your iPhone. It is not synced to a server and we keep no copy. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or a computer, it is part of that backup, protected by Apple. It is deleted when you delete the app, or in the app with Settings › Erase Everything.

Export in Settings makes a file (JSON or Markdown) only when you ask, and it goes only where you send it.

Widgets, Siri and Shortcuts

Widgets, Control Center and Live Activities read a short summary of your day that Daycamp keeps on the same iPhone. Siri and Shortcuts actions run on your iPhone.

Apple Health (optional)

If you connect Apple Health, Daycamp reads steps, exercise minutes, active energy, sleep, mindful minutes and weight to complete your habits and show your day, and saves water, nutrition, weight, focus sessions (as mindful minutes) and mood to Health. Health data stays on your iPhone and in Health. It is never used for advertising and is never sent to us or anyone else. You can change what Daycamp may read or write in Settings › Privacy & Security › Health.

Screen Time (optional)

Daycamp uses Apple's Screen Time framework to set a daily limit for the apps you choose and to block them during focus sessions. The apps you choose and your usage are handled by iOS and shown inside Apple's own protected report; Daycamp cannot send them anywhere.

Location (optional)

If you set up an automation for a place, iOS tells Daycamp when you arrive at, leave or stay at that place, so a habit can be reminded, logged or timed. Daycamp keeps the places you save (a name, a point on the map and a distance) and whether you are inside one. It does not keep a history of where you have been, and your location is never sent anywhere.

Calendar and Reminders (optional)

Calendar events are read to show them in your plan. Reminders you pick are imported as tasks. Daycamp does not change your calendars or reminders.

Camera and photos

The camera is used only to scan food barcodes; frames are read on your iPhone and nothing is recorded. Photos for journal entries are chosen with Apple's photo picker, which gives Daycamp only the photos you select; copies are kept with the entry on your iPhone.

Notifications and Face ID

Reminders are scheduled on your iPhone; there is no notification server. If you lock the app, iOS checks Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode; Daycamp never sees biometric data.

Online food search

When you search foods under Online, or scan a barcode, the words you typed or the barcode number are sent to Open Food Facts (openfoodfacts.org) to look up nutrition values, with the app's name and version. Nothing else is sent: no name, no identifier and no data from your journal or habits. As with any web request, their server sees your IP address. The Open Food Facts privacy policy applies to that request. The built-in food list and your recent foods work without it.

Purchases

Daycamp Premium subscriptions and the lifetime purchase are processed by Apple through the App Store. We never receive your payment details or your Apple Account. See Apple's privacy policy for how Apple handles purchase data.

Children

Daycamp collects no data from anyone, including children.
